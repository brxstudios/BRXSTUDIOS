# Copy the menu, not the shortcuts

There San Diego (theresandiego.com) is a founder-led "things to eat, drink, see and do" guide that **runs more like a local marketing agency than a newsroom**. It is published by There Media Group, LLC under founder and CEO Mike Cooch since about 2016. It rests on a few evergreen URLs that are rewritten every week or month: The Craving (food, Tuesdays), the weekend Big List (Thursdays), Things to Do This Month, Top Concerts This Week, and a monthly new-restaurants tracker. Around those sit "Best X Right Now (2026 Guide)" pillar pages and a WordPress events calendar. A twice-weekly newsletter claims 25,000–30,000 subscribers, and Instagram and Facebook each have about 41,000–42,000 followers. The site earns money from a self-serve ad menu priced at **$97 / $297 / $497 / $997 / $1,500**: sponsored stories boosted with paid Meta ads, newsletter slots, social posts and giveaways, deal listings, event boosts and directory profiles. The model transfers well to Saratoga Springs. The local market has a **$900M+ county visitor economy**, a racing meet with **more than 1 million paid admissions a year**, a cut-back daily paper, a magazine whose calendar newsletter is suspended, and no modern weekly "what to do" newsletter for locals. HereSaratoga should copy San Diego's series structure and product ladder. It should not copy the weak spots: stale pages, contradictory audience claims and unclear sponsored labeling. Two adjustments matter most. Prices should start at roughly 40–60% of San Diego's, and the plan should treat November–April as the time to build the audience and **June–Labor Day 2027 as the time to earn the revenue**. One caveat applies to everything below. **None of the theresandiego.com, offers. or advertise. pages could be fetched directly** (DNS failures; archive.org was blocked too). Every San Diego finding comes from search-engine titles, URLs and summaries of indexed text gathered on 2026-10-19. Prices, subscriber counts and cadences in particular must be checked on the live site before anyone relies on them.

## Four rewritten URLs and a long tail of guides make up the site

The site is organized around a small set of hubs: Eat + Drink, See/Do/Events (titled "The Best Things To Do In San Diego"), Guides, Free & Easy and Giveaways. It also has an `/event/` archive, `/venue/` pages and `/location/` taxonomies ([Eat + Drink](https://theresandiego.com/eat-drink/); [See Do Events](https://theresandiego.com/see-do-events/); [Events Archive](https://theresandiego.com/event/)). The homepage paginates to at least `/page/192/`, and old archive pages reach `/page/286/`. That points to a deep reverse-chronological feed built up over about ten years ([Page 192](https://theresandiego.com/page/192/?mc_eid=2f19e94ec8); [Page 286](https://theresandiego.com/page/286/?mc_eid=4f5f495254)). The exact menu labels and the order of homepage modules could not be seen.

The editorial engine is the **"living post" pattern: one fixed slug that is overwritten on a schedule**, so search authority builds on the URL while the newsletter keeps linking back to it. **The Craving**, "the weekly guide to what's worth eating and drinking in San Diego right now", publishes on Tuesdays under "Week of …" headers. It mixes openings, happy hours, limited-time menu items and food events, with practical details such as addresses and hours ([The Craving](https://theresandiego.com/the-craving-san-diego-restaurants-and-bars/)). **The Big List of Things to Do This Weekend** says it is sent "to 25,000 locals... free in your inbox every Thursday" ([This Weekend](https://theresandiego.com/things-to-do-in-san-diego-this-weekend/)). The monthly list keeps the slug `/things-to-do-in-san-diego-this-month/` and is retitled each month ([This Month](https://theresandiego.com/things-to-do-in-san-diego-this-month/)). There is also a weekly concerts list ([Concerts](https://theresandiego.com/concerts-in-san-diego-this-week/)). The "New Restaurants Opening in San Diego in 2026 (Updated Monthly)" tracker had reached 24 spots by early August ([New Restaurants 2026](https://theresandiego.com/new-restaurants-opening-in-san-diego-in-2026/)). In earlier years the site created a new dated `/event/` URL for each weekend, and it has since moved away from that ([legacy weekend post](https://theresandiego.com/event/the-great-big-list-of-things-to-do-this-weekend-in-san-diego-march23-march26/)). Smaller series include **SD Originals** founder and character profiles and an "Escapes:" getaway label ([SD Originals](https://theresandiego.com/sd-originals-hackett-dave/); [Idyllwild](https://theresandiego.com/san-diego-road-trip-guide-to-idyllwild/)).

The evergreen layer follows a repeatable formula: **"The Best [X] in San Diego Right Now (2026 Guide)" plus a "Last updated: [Month Year]" stamp**. Pages in this format cover restaurants, brunch, bars, rooftop bars and cocktail bars ([Best Restaurants](https://theresandiego.com/best-restaurants-in-san-diego/); [Brunch](https://theresandiego.com/the-best-brunch-in-san-diego-right-now-2026-guide/); [Bars](https://theresandiego.com/the-best-bars-in-san-diego-right-now-2026-guide/)). There is also a "Local's Guide (2026)" things-to-do pillar ([Things to Do](https://theresandiego.com/things-to-do-in-san-diego/)) and a single neighborhood guide, for North Park ([North Park](https://theresandiego.com/north-park-san-diego-the-complete-neighborhood-guide/)). Further down are day-trip listicles ("17 Amazing Road Trips", "14 Must-Do Day Trips") and seasonal roundups that come back each year, covering Halloween, pumpkin patches, Thanksgiving, Christmas lights, NYE, Valentine's Day and July 4th ([17 Road Trips](https://theresandiego.com/17-road-trips-from-san-diego/); [Halloween](https://theresandiego.com/san-diego-halloween-events-food-drinks-and-fun/); [Christmas lights](https://theresandiego.com/top-neighborhoods-to-view-christmas-lights-in-san-diego/)). Family and free content, such as "Bring the Kids to 60+ Museums Free This October", fills the Free & Easy niche ([Kids Free](https://theresandiego.com/kids-free-october-san-diego-museum-council/)). A steady stream of single-venue opening posts (Lana, Kura, Rare Society, North Italia and others) serves as both news and, very likely, sponsored inventory ([Lana](https://theresandiego.com/lana-solana-beach-restaurant-highway-101/); [North Italia](https://theresandiego.com/north-italia-one-paseo-now-open/)).

Events coverage combines a WordPress calendar with editorial roundups. Single event posts include Art After Hours, the North Park Thursday Market Halloween and the USS Midway workout. Alongside them are bare-name listings such as "Dread Mar I" and "Chris Tomlin" that look imported from a feed ([Art After Hours](https://theresandiego.com/event/exclusive-evening-access-sd-museum-art-art-after-hours/); [Chris Tomlin](https://theresandiego.com/event/chris-tomlin/)). The `/event/` and `/venue/` slug pair matches the default permalinks of The Events Calendar plugin. That is an inference, not a confirmed fact. Tips come in through tips@theresandiego.com, and no public submission form, paid-listing rule or calendar view was found ([Contact Us](https://theresandiego.com/contactus/)).

The voice is a **casual, first-person-plural local insider with firm opinions**. Soichi is "not cheap and you should book weeks ahead", The Taco Stand's line "always moves fast", and the Gaslamp is "Loud, expensive, and fun" ([Best Restaurants](https://theresandiego.com/best-restaurants-in-san-diego/); [Things to Do](https://theresandiego.com/things-to-do-in-san-diego/)). That voice is the hardest part to reproduce with AI drafting, which comes up again below.

## The newsletter and Instagram are the audience; the ad menu is the business

According to the About page, the newsletter "lands in your inbox twice a week" on Tuesdays and Thursdays ([About](https://theresandiego.com/about/)). An `mc_eid` parameter in indexed URLs points to Mailchimp as the sender ([Page 192](https://theresandiego.com/page/192/?mc_eid=2f19e94ec8)). The site's own subscriber claims **contradict each other: "nearly 20,000" on the older packages page, "over 25,000" on the advertise page and in the restaurant guide, and "nearly 30,000+" on the newsletter-ads page** ([Packages](https://theresandiego.com/packages/); [Advertise](https://theresandiego.com/advertise/); [Newsletter Ads](https://offers.theresandiego.com/newsletter-ads-page)). The most likely explanation is slow organic growth over several years, with pages never brought up to date. No open rate is published. A comparable San Diego publisher, SanDiegoVille, reports a 37% average open rate ([SanDiegoVille media kit](https://www.sandiegoville.com/p/advertise.html)).

On social, Instagram @theresandiego has about **42K followers across about 3,081 posts**, and Facebook has **41,306 followers** ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/theresandiego/); [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/theresandiego/)). The X account is about ten years old and looks dormant ([X](https://x.com/ThereSanDiego)). No TikTok or YouTube account was confirmed. About 3,000 posts over ten years is fewer than one feed post a day, which suggests Stories and sponsored posts carry much of the load. That is an inference that was not checked against the feed. The headline audience claim is "over 70,000 unique visitors" a month and "700,000+ people across web, email, and social", with a **76% female, 75% aged 25–44** skew ([Advertise](https://theresandiego.com/advertise/)). The restaurant guide gives a different web figure, "40,000–80,000 monthly readers" ([Best Restaurants](https://theresandiego.com/best-restaurants-in-san-diego/)). The 700K reach figure is roughly ten times the uniques, is self-reported, and was not verified by any third party (no Similarweb or Quantcast data was found).

Monetization is a productized, mostly self-serve menu. It is sold through advertise.theresandiego.com and per-product landing pages on offers.theresandiego.com, some of which show booking-calendar dates ([Advertise hub](https://advertise.theresandiego.com/); [Social Ads](https://offers.theresandiego.com/SocialAds-page)). The core offer is the **Sponsored Story**. The team writes an article about the business, publishes it, and promotes it "with targeted Facebook and Instagram ads", backed by a **monthly impression guarantee** ([Sponsored Stories](https://offers.theresandiego.com/SponsoredStories-page)). In effect, the company resells boosted Meta reach wrapped in branded content. The guarantee covers impressions, not clicks, which makes it easy to fulfil with ad spend.

## A one-founder operation dressed as a media brand

The privacy policy names **There Media Group, LLC** as the publisher ([Privacy](https://theresandiego.com/privacy/)). Mike Cooch is listed as publisher and CEO. He is a self-described "4x founder" with a Babson MBA, and he also runs LVRG, a business-education brand for owner-operators ([CreativeMornings](https://creativemornings.com/individuals/mcooch); [LVRG About](https://lvrg.com/about/)). His personal site describes the company as "the modern local media and marketing solutions business to serve the San Diego community first, then to the moon" ([mikecooch.com](https://mikecooch.com/)). Aggregator profiles and a copyright line point to a **launch around 2016** ([ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/p/Michael-Cooch/1875521400)). A 2020 podcast still introduced it as a "new online publication" ([BizSoup](https://bizsoup.com/075-mike-cooch/)). San Diego Magazine's influencer roundup, from about 2023, credits Cooch with "35,000-plus followers" and frames the account as one that promotes small businesses ([San Diego Magazine](https://sandiegomagazine.com/food-drink/san-diego-food-influencers-to-follow-right-now/)).

The operation looks lean. Most articles carry a **house byline ("There SanDiego")**, and only one named freelancer, Francine Tupas, has been confirmed ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/theresandiego)). Contributors are recruited through contribute@theresandiego.com ([Contact](https://theresandiego.com/contact-us/)). The listed addresses have moved from a unit on Pacific Place to an apartment-complex unit at 3200 Paseo Village Way, with a Del Mar address on MapQuest. That pattern fits a home or virtual office ([Apartments.com](https://www.apartments.com/one-paseo-living-san-diego-ca/djmcwtk/); [MapQuest](https://www.mapquest.com/us/california/there-media-group-llc-388351541)). No sister "There[City]" sites were found. Independent reputation signals are close to absent. Searches turned up no Reddit threads and no reviews, which suggests locals treat it as a feed and a deals newsletter rather than a source they cite.

The technology is probably **WordPress with an events-calendar plugin, Mailchimp for email, and a separate funnel or booking builder** on the offers subdomain. Its "-page" slugs, booking dates and leftover template text about "lead-management software" fit a tool like GoHighLevel. That last point is a guess and was not verified ([Calendar](https://offers.theresandiego.com/calendar)).

## Strengths to copy and weaknesses to avoid

The strengths add up to a sensible system. Rewritten-in-place URLs build search authority and give the newsletter fixed places to link. The "Right Now (Year Guide)" plus "Last updated" format signals freshness to readers and to search engines. A Tuesday food send and a Thursday weekend send match the two moments in the week when locals make plans. The ad menu has clear prices, can be booked without a sales call, and bundles site, social, newsletter and paid Meta reach into one purchase, which a small business can understand. Because the guarantee is in impressions bought on Meta, it can be delivered at any audience size. That matters a great deal for a new site with a small list.

The weaknesses are just as instructive. Stale content is still live and indexed: NYE pages aimed at 2025, a COVID-era outdoor dining page, and duplicate holiday URLs such as the two "Kids Free" posts ([NYE](https://theresandiego.com/nye-parties-san-diego/); [Outdoor dining](https://theresandiego.com/outdoor-dining-san-diego-restaurants/)). Audience claims contradict each other from page to page, which undercuts trust with advertisers. Offer pages show 2022–2023 dates and placeholder text. Paid venue features look the same as editorial in title style, and **no on-page sponsored labeling could be verified**. The pay-to-feature model also caps credibility: the site does not show up in the places locals go for recommendations, unlike critic-led outlets. A Saratoga copy can fix every one of these from the start at almost no cost.

## Saratoga's big summer, thin newsroom and open lane

The market is small in residents but large in visitors. Saratoga Springs has about **28,500–28,700 residents** ([Census QuickFacts](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/saratogaspringscitynewyork/PST045222); [Data USA](https://maine.datausa.io/profile/geo/16000US3665255)). It sits in a growing county of **240,360** ([Census county](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/saratogacountynewyork/PST045224)) and a metro of **913,485** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ALBPOP)). County visitor spending **topped $900M in 2024** ([Discover Saratoga](https://www.discoversaratoga.org/articles/post/saratoga-county-tourism-tops-900-million-in-2024-visitor-spending/)). In 2023, **food and beverage was the largest category at about $318M** ([NYSTIA](https://www.nystia.org/about/tourism-advocacy/nys-economic-impact/saratoga)), which supports a food-led guide. The 2026 summer meet drew **1,045,373 paid admissions, the 11th straight year above 1M, and $905.4M in handle**. Counting the Belmont Stakes Racing Festival, the total was 1,149,677 over 50 days ([NYRA](https://www.nyra.com/saratoga/news/nyra-concludes-summer-meet-at-saratoga-race-course/)). The Belmont is being held at Saratoga while Belmont Park is rebuilt, which stretches "track season" into a June festival plus the July–Labor Day meet ([CBS6](https://www.cbs6albany.com/news/local/saratoga-to-host-july-4th-racing-festival-as-construction-alters-nyra-schedule-belmont-park-racing-horse-race-festival-stakes-purse-betting-); [NYRA 2026 schedule](https://www.nyra.com/saratoga/news/nyra-announces-2026-racing-schedule-multi-year-agreement-on-race-dates/)). Whether that arrangement covers 2027 should be confirmed against NYRA's 2027 calendar. Summer is so concentrated that one hotel reported occupancy about 20% higher on July racing days, although that figure is old ([Glens Falls Business Journal](https://www.glensfalls.com/glensfallsbusinessjournal/2015/04/report-track-season-means-237m-and-2600-jobs-for-the-greater-capital-region)). Restaurant churn is active, which gives an openings tracker plenty to cover. 2026 saw Elody open on Phila St, Noah's planned from the Siro's team, Familiar Creature and Bibulous announced, Viva Empanadas open on Congress St and Gaffney's sold to reopen ([Gazette](https://www.dailygazette.com/food/new-capital-region-restaurants/article_1d797d60-c92b-11ef-9dbd-23546afa361a.html); [News10](https://www.news10.com/community/restaurant-rundown/latin-eatery-opens-third-location-in-saratoga-springs/amp/)).

The competitive field is crowded but fragmented, and no one occupies the There San Diego position.

## How HereSaratoga should replicate the model

### The proposed names and scope hold up, with three adjustments

**"The Morning Line"** is a strong newsletter name. In racing, the morning line is the track's early read on how a race will go, which maps neatly onto "our early read on your weekend." It signals local fluency on day one. The risk is that for seven months a year it reads as racing-only to locals who never go to the track. The tagline and the first line of each issue should therefore make clear it covers food, events and weekends year-round. Run a trademark and existing-use search before committing, because the phrase is common in racing media. **"The Feedbag"** works well as the Craving equivalent: it is a horse pun that reads as food without any explanation. The **Track Season hub** is the most important single page on the site, and the attendance and spending data justify it. **County-wide coverage** is right for the openings tracker, events and Deal Ads radius tiers, since the county has more than eight times the city's population. The brand and the pillar guides should still be centered on Saratoga Springs, with Ballston Spa, Malta, Wilton, Clifton Park and Schuylerville as named spokes. This mirrors San Diego's Local / Citywide / Countywide sponsored tiers, which map directly onto Downtown / Saratoga Springs / Saratoga County.

### AI drafting needs guardrails San Diego never published

AI can handle the structured work: weekly event aggregation, openings-tracker entries, hours, addresses and first-draft listicles. It cannot produce the thing that makes There San Diego's guides useful, which is first-hand judgment ("book weeks ahead"). Every "Best X" pillar and every Feedbag pick needs a named human who has actually been there, and every fact (dates, prices, hours, whether a place is open) needs a human check against a primary source before publishing. AI drafts should never generate quotes, menu prices or "we tried it" claims. Publish an AI and fact-check policy, a visible "Last verified" date, and a correction line. Label sponsored content in the headline area and the newsletter slot, and keep paid posts out of the "Best X" rankings. Use venue-supplied photos with written permission, licensed stock and the site's own photos. Do not use AI-generated images that depict real venues or dishes. Together these steps turn San Diego's credibility weakness into a selling point for HereSaratoga.

### Revenue products and starting prices (all prices are planning assumptions, not market data)

The rule behind these numbers is to keep San Diego's ladder shape and its impression-guarantee mechanism, and to start at roughly 40–60% of its prices. The reasons: a launch list in the low thousands, an unproven brand, and a 28.5K-resident core. Prices should step up during track season and as the list grows. San Diego's implied CPMs of $30–50 are a sanity check for impression-guaranteed products.

## Conclusion

There San Diego's real product is not journalism but **a dependable distribution bundle: a fixed weekly rhythm, a list, an Instagram account, and Meta ad spend wrapped in local storytelling**, sold at prices a café owner can say yes to without a meeting. That is good news for HereSaratoga. The bundle works at small audience sizes because paid reach fills the impression guarantees, so revenue can begin before the list is large. The Saratoga version will most likely succeed or fail on its off-season. Track season will sell itself if the Track Season hub and the Morning Line are already trusted by May 2027. Trust is where HereSaratoga can beat its model rather than copy it. Clear sponsored labels, dated audience claims, stale-page retirement and visible human verification of AI drafts cost almost nothing, and they are exactly where There San Diego is weakest. The most urgent next step is the one this research could not do: open theresandiego.com, its offers. pages and its Instagram on a live connection and confirm current prices, cadences, subscriber counts and sponsored labeling. After that, call Discover Saratoga and Saratoga Living for their rate cards so the price assumptions above can be replaced with local anchors.
