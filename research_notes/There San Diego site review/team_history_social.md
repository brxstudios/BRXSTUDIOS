# There San Diego (ThereSanDiego.com): People, History, and Social Media Presence

Research method note: as of 2026-10-19, WebFetch failed with DNS errors (ENOTFOUND) for every domain tried: theresandiego.com, muckrack.com, whirlocal.io, bizsoup.com, mikecooch.com and sandiegomagazine.com. web.archive.org is blocked. Every finding below comes from search-engine snippets and summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the pages in full. Snapshot ages reported by the search tool are noted where they matter. Treat the numbers as approximate and check them directly before relying on them.

## Who runs it: founders, owners, editors, writers, company name, launch date, sister sites

### Takeaway
There San Diego is published by There Media Group, LLC. Its founder, publisher and CEO is Mike (Michael) Cooch, a serial entrepreneur based in San Diego who has an MBA from Babson College. The company appears to date from about 2016. Most articles are credited to a house account ("ThereSanDiego" / "There SanDiego"), and only a few named freelancers have been found. I found no evidence of sister "There[City]" sites. The deals and offers business runs as a subdomain of the same brand.

### Inferences
- The operation looks founder-led and lean: one owner who acts as publisher and salesperson, a house byline, and freelance or contributed content sourced through a contribute@ inbox. No masthead turned up, which suggests a very small core team.
- Cooch presents ThereSanDiego as a marketing-services business that wears a media brand ("media and marketing solutions"), not as journalism. That framing matters for a Saratoga Springs version.
- The name "There Media Group" and the "then to the moon" line suggest he planned a multi-city "There[City]" network, but nothing shows one exists.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn profile of Cooch was retrieved directly. His career details come from aggregators and his own sites.

- No full list of editors or staff, and no team-size figure. Francine Tupas is the only named contributor confirmed, and their role and dates are unclear.
- I could not confirm the existence of other "There[City]" sites.
- ZoomInfo, RocketReach and similar sites list possible co-owners, investors and Cooch's personal details. I did not use the personal details, and they are unverified.

## History, growth milestones, press, interviews, podcasts

### Takeaway
The public record is thin. The clearest milestones are a launch around 2016, social follower counts in the tens of thousands by about 2023 (35K+, per San Diego Magazine), an October 2020 podcast interview positioning it as a "new online publication," and a move toward packaged advertising, deals and newsletter products with published price lists in 2023–2026.

### Cited Findings
- Biz Soup Podcast Radio, Episode 75, "Michael Cooch: The New World of Content and Content Distribution" (Oct 23, 2020, 22 min). Host John DeBevoise introduces Cooch as founder of a new online publication, and the episode covers how the site fits into digital marketing. The episode page says the company's clients have included "bars and restaurants, events, banks, lawyers" — [Amazon Music episode](https://music.amazon.com/podcasts/738abeaf-9348-4a1e-a99a-45154da71ab3/episodes/70bd6fb9-224c-4f29-a5f2-59bdeed2cbdd/biz-soup-podcast-radio-michael-cooch-the-new-world-of-content-and-content-distribution); [BizSoup page](https://bizsoup.com/075-mike-cooch/); [Apple Podcasts show](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/biz-soup-podcast-radio/id1491389131)

- Press: San Diego Magazine included @ThereSanDiego / Mike Cooch in a roundup of San Diego food influencers, about 2023 — [San Diego Magazine](https://sandiegomagazine.com/food-drink/san-diego-food-influencers-to-follow-right-now/)
- Advertising agreement terms are dated January 1, 2023 — [Advertising Terms](https://theresandiego.com/advertisingterms/)
- The newsletter subscriber figures the site itself claims rose from "nearly 20,000" on the packages page, to "over 25,000" with "40,000–80,000 monthly readers" on the advertise page and restaurant guide, to "nearly 30,000+" on the newsletter-ads page — [Packages](https://theresandiego.com/packages/); [Advertise](https://theresandiego.com/advertise/); [Newsletter Ads](https://offers.theresandiego.com/newsletter-ads-page). ### Inferences
- Growth seems to have come from building social and email audiences first, then turning them into money through standardized self-serve ad packages. The 2023 terms and the tiered price pages point to a shift toward productized selling.
- The steady rise in subscriber claims (20K → 25K → 30K+) points to slow, organic list growth over several years, not a breakout moment.

### Gaps

## Social presence: handles, follower counts, content types, how social drives traffic

### Takeaway
Instagram (@theresandiego, about 42K followers, about 3,081 posts) and Facebook (/theresandiego, about 41K followers) are the main channels. X (@ThereSanDiego) exists but looks inactive or legacy. I could not confirm TikTok or YouTube accounts. Social works as both audience and inventory: brands buy Instagram giveaway posts, stories and Facebook shares as packaged ads, and these feed the newsletter and the site.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram @theresandiego ("THERESanDiego · San Diego, CA"): about 42K followers, 3,701 following, 3,081 posts. The snapshot date is unknown — [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/theresandiego/)
- Facebook "There San Diego": 41,306 followers, 371 talking about this. The snapshot is undated — [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/theresandiego/)
- X @ThereSanDiego bio: "your source for all of the best things to eat, drink, see & do in San Diego!" Search metadata marks it about ten years old, and no follower count was shown — [X](https://x.com/ThereSanDiego)
- San Diego Magazine cited "35,000-plus followers" (about 2023) without naming the platform — [San Diego Magazine](https://sandiegomagazine.com/food-drink/san-diego-food-influencers-to-follow-right-now/)
- Giveaways as a paid product: the ad packages include "Instagram post with tag and follow giveaway contest live for 7 days," with Instagram stories and a Facebook share — [Packages](https://theresandiego.com/packages/). The site has a /giveaway/ hub, which showed "No Giveaways Available At The Moment" when indexed — [Giveaways](https://theresandiego.com/giveaway/)
- Recent promoted contests include a Fandiem/Curebound sweepstakes for a VIP trip to see P!NK at Petco Park, and a "San Diego Vintage Crawl: Win Rewards as You Shop" — [P!NK sweepstakes](https://theresandiego.com/fandiem-sweepstakes-concert-for-cures-vip-giveaway/); [Vintage Crawl](https://theresandiego.com/san-diego-vintage-crawl/)
- Content verticals include Eat + Drink, best-of guides, weekly deals, events and restaurant-week roundups — [Eat + Drink](https://theresandiego.com/eat-drink/); [Restaurant Week deals](https://theresandiego.com/7-cant-miss-san-diego-restaurant-week-deals-to-take-advantage-of-but-you-better-hurry/)
- Pricing (from the site's own pages, which are inconsistent and may be dated):
  - Newsletter ads. Packages page: $397 for one, $750 for three, $2,369 for twelve (promo). Newsletter-ads page: $497 for one weekly placement, $997 for three, $1,500 for a dedicated email sent twice.
  - Monthly plans: Engage $497, Impact $997, Influence $1,500 (also labeled Local, Citywide and Countywide for sponsored stories).
  - Business Profile listing: $297, one-time.
  - Deals tiers run from a free listing to "Featured" at $497/month with 10,000 guaranteed impressions, and the company says it "never takes a percentage."
  - Restaurant-specific packages also exist.
  - Sources: [Packages](https://theresandiego.com/packages/); [Newsletter Ads](https://offers.theresandiego.com/newsletter-ads-page); [Sponsored Stories](https://offers.theresandiego.com/SponsoredStories-page); [Deal Ads](https://offers.theresandiego.com/get-deals-page); [Restaurant Packages](https://theresandiego.com/restaurant-packages/)
- The offers.theresandiego.com/social page markets deals tied to social distribution — [Deals – Learn More](https://offers.theresandiego.com/social)

### Inferences
- The model bundles reach across channels: one advertiser purchase covers an Instagram feed post, stories, a Facebook share and a newsletter or site placement. Social is the most visible and saleable piece, while the newsletter and SEO guides (such as "Best Restaurants 2026") bring in steady traffic.
- About 3,000 Instagram posts over roughly ten years works out to under one feed post a day, so the account probably leans on Stories and sponsored posts rather than daily original reels. This was not verified by viewing the feed.

### Gaps
- TikTok and YouTube: I found no confirmed accounts or follower counts. Searches for @theresandiego on TikTok and YouTube returned nothing.
- The reel-versus-static mix, engagement rates and recent posting frequency are unverified, because Instagram could not be fetched.
- There is no data on how much site traffic comes from social versus search versus email.

## Reputation: reviews, Reddit, criticism, and comparison with competitors

### Takeaway
I found almost no independent reputation signals. Searches of Reddit and review sites turned up no indexed threads about There San Diego, and I found no criticism. The one third-party editorial mention (San Diego Magazine, about 2023) is positive and frames it as an influencer account that promotes small businesses. By its own pricing pages, it is positioned as an advertiser-friendly promotional outlet, not an independent critic like Eater or the Infatuation.

### Cited Findings
- San Diego Magazine's positive mention: a "boon to visitors planning itineraries" that promotes small businesses — [San Diego Magazine](https://sandiegomagazine.com/food-drink/san-diego-food-influencers-to-follow-right-now/)
- WhirLocal hosts a "ThereSanDiego.com Reviews" page, but its contents could not be retrieved — [WhirLocal](https://whirlocal.io/company/theresandiego/)
- Searches for "theresandiego reddit" and for There San Diego reviews or opinions returned no relevant Reddit or review results (searches run 2026-10-19).
- Competitor context: San Diego Magazine was acquired in October 2021 by writer and Food Network host Troy Johnson and his wife Claire — [Wikipedia: San Diego Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Magazine). The San Diego Reader is an alternative weekly published since October 1972 — [Wikipedia: San Diego Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Reader)
- There San Diego's sponsored stories, paid giveaways and "deal ads" are sold openly as packages — [Packages](https://theresandiego.com/packages/); [Sponsored Stories](https://offers.theresandiego.com/SponsoredStories-page)
- A similarly named but separate account, @wheresandiego (Lisa Kelley, "WHERE to go and what to do in San Diego"), exists and could easily be confused with @theresandiego — [Starngage](https://starngage.com/spotlight/instagram-influencer-spotlight-on-lisa-kelley-wheresandiego/)

### Inferences
- The low Reddit visibility suggests locals treat it more as a social feed or deals newsletter than as an authority people cite in recommendation threads. Eater, the Infatuation, the Reader and San Diego Magazine are critic- or newsroom-driven, while There San Diego works on a pay-to-feature, small-business-marketing model.
- Its main peer is probably other independent "things to do" newsletters and Instagram accounts such as Hello San Diego, not the legacy outlets.

### Gaps
- No direct comparisons with Hello San Diego, Time Out, Eater San Diego or the Infatuation were found in sources. Any comparison would be inference.
- No Google or Yelp business reviews for There Media Group were found.
- Reddit could not be fetched directly, so thread-level sentiment remains unknown.
