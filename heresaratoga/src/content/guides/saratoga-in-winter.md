Everyone knows Saratoga in August. Locals will tell you the city is just as good from November to March — maybe better. Hotel prices drop, you can walk into restaurants without a reservation, and the town throws some of its best parties of the year.

## The winter calendar

### Victorian Streetwalk (early December)

The holiday season starts with the Victorian Streetwalk: a tree lighting, carolers and costumed performers along Broadway, with shops open late. In recent years it has been the first Thursday in December. See the [events calendar](/events/) for this year’s date.

### New Year’s Fest (December 31)

The city’s New Year’s celebration — formerly First Night — fills downtown venues with concerts and family events, with fireworks to finish. Tickets for the indoor shows usually go on sale in late November.

### Chowderfest (early February)

The biggest winter day of the year. Dozens of restaurants serve chowder by the cup and everyone votes for the best. Go early, wear good boots and bring cash. Check the [calendar](/events/) for the date.

## Things to do on a cold day

- **Take a mineral bath** at the Roosevelt Baths & Spa in [Saratoga Spa State Park](/guides/saratoga-spa-state-park/).
- **Ski or snowshoe** the state park trails after a snowfall.
- **Catch a show at Caffè Lena**, the legendary folk coffeehouse, which runs shows year-round.
- **Browse Broadway** — start at Northshire Bookstore and work your way north.
- **Go to a museum** — the [racing museum](/explore/things-to-do/) is open in winter, and it’s much quieter than in August.

## Where to eat

Winter is the best time to try the restaurants everyone lines up for in summer. Our [eat & drink list](/explore/eat-drink/) is the place to start.

## Weekend getaway

Saratoga is about three and a half hours from New York City and an hour and a half from the Adirondacks. Make it a winter long weekend: Spa State Park on Saturday, Broadway on Saturday night, and a slow Sunday brunch.
