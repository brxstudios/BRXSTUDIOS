Before the horses, before the ballet, there was the water. Saratoga Springs exists because of the naturally carbonated mineral springs that bubble up from deep underground. For two centuries people came here to drink them and bathe in them for their health. You still can — for free.

## What does it taste like?

Honestly? It’s an acquired taste. Most of the springs are fizzy, salty and metallic, sometimes with a hint of sulfur. Some are strong enough to make you wince. That’s part of the fun. Bring a cup or a reusable bottle, try a few and pick a favorite.

> A note of caution: mineral content varies a lot from spring to spring, and some are very high in sodium. Take a sip, not a jug.

## Where to find them

### Downtown

- **Congress Spring**, in Congress Park, is the most famous and the easiest to find — start here.
- **Hathorn Spring**, near Congress Park, is one of the strongest-tasting.
- **High Rock Spring**, in High Rock Park, is where it all started. The Mohawk knew it long before Europeans arrived, and Sir William Johnson was famously brought here in the 1700s to take the waters.

### Saratoga Spa State Park

- **State Seal Spring** is the mildest, most drinkable water in the city. Locals fill jugs here.
- **Geyser Creek** has a handful of springs along a short trail, including the **Island Spouter**, which shoots water from a crusted orange mound. Look, don’t drink.
- The park’s historic **Hall of Springs** was built so visitors could drink the water in style.

## Make it a walk

You can see several downtown springs in an easy hour: start at Congress Park, walk to High Rock Park, and stop for coffee on Broadway in between. Then head to Spa State Park to finish with State Seal and Geyser Creek. A local visitor map with every spring is available from Discover Saratoga.

## Then go soak in it

Drinking is half the experience. At the **Roosevelt Baths & Spa** in Spa State Park, you can still take a mineral bath the way visitors have for generations.
